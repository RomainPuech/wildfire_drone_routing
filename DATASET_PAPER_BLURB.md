Each layout stores three risk-related products and Sim2Real-Fire spread masks aligned to a 30 m grid. Static USDA Burn Probability (`static_risk_bp2024.npy`, legacy name `static_risk.npy`) is released as FSim-scaled integers (annual probability × 10 000; divide by 10 000 for \(r\in[0,1]\)), while Wildfire Hazard Potential (`static_risk_whp.npy`) is a USFS integer hazard index used as a relative weight rather than a calibrated probability. The dynamic evaluation map (`burn_map.npy`) is a float32 tensor in \([0,1]\) giving, for each hour and cell, the fraction of simulated scenarios with fire after binarizing JPG masks (grayscale/255, threshold \(\ge 0.5\); preprocessed `scenarii/*.npy` is canonical when present). The benchmark loaders in `code/dataset.py` read arrays as stored; before optimization, `code/benchmark.py` spatially pools maps to the drone coverage grid and temporally subdivides each hour—without re-scaling USFS integer units—while Julia optimizers consume the resulting `.npy` paths (`julia/helper_functions.jl`, `julia/ground_charging_opt.jl`, `julia/drone_routing_opt.jl`). The paper’s \(r_{it}\in[0,1]\) notation denotes this probabilistic model interface; on-disk encodings follow source-product units unless already normalized (as with `burn_map.npy`).
